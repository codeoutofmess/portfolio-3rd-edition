<Step n="01" title="The idea">
A grid of thumbnails can only point at what a drone filmmaker sells: how a place looks from above and how smoothly the camera moves through it. So the navigation became the sample. You take off and fly to each section.
</Step>

<Media src="/images/work/fly-eire/flight-hud.jpg" caption="The HUD: timecode, compass, altitude and a radar that finds the landmarks." />

<Step n="02" title="Five places">
Archive sits at the Cliffs of Moher, Services at the Giant's Causeway, Grade Lab in Kerry, Gear in the Wicklow Mountains and bookings in Dublin.
</Step>

<Pair>
  <Media src="/images/work/fly-eire/menu.jpg" caption="The landmark menu is always one click away." />
  <Media src="/images/work/fly-eire/phone.jpg" caption="Phones get a joystick and tap to fly." />
</Pair>

<Step n="03" title="Four tries at the world">
A procedural coastline looked like an old video game. Photoreal map tiles needed a billing account. A photo with a depth map tore apart once you flew. The fourth try, a printed style relief map from real elevation data, stuck because it doesn't pretend to be anything else.
</Step>

<Media src="/images/work/fly-eire/pipeline.svg" caption="Elevation tiles are packed into one heightmap, and a shader draws the relief." />

<Step n="04" title="Still easy to use">
Click a pin and the drone flies there. A menu and a Book a date button stay on screen, and without WebGL the menu opens each section directly.
</Step>

<Media src="/images/work/fly-eire/cover.jpg" caption="The start screen." />

<Step n="05" title="Where it stands">
Live on a temporary address while it's in development. The films, prices and dates are placeholders for him to confirm.
</Step>
